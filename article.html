How to build accessible pages

A short, practical checklist you can apply to every page before it ships.

By Sam Rivera 3 November 2026 6 min read

Accessible pages are better pages. They load faster, read more clearly and work for everyone, whatever device or ability they bring. Here are the habits that matter most.

Start with semantics

Choose the element that matches the meaning of the content: a button for actions, a link for navigation, a list for lists. The browser then gives you keyboard support and screen reader behaviour for free.

  • One h1 per page, and headings that never skip a level
  • Landmarks: header, nav, main, footer
  • Real label elements for every form field

Check contrast and focus

Text needs enough contrast against its background in both light and dark themes. The framework pairs every colour with a readable text colour, so use those pairs instead of picking by eye.

If you can't see where the keyboard focus is, neither can your users.

Targets and spacing

Keep interactive targets at least 24 by 24 pixels, and aim for 44 where you can.

Respect motion preferences

Write animation so it only runs for people who haven't asked for reduced motion. Fades are fine; sliding, zooming and parallax are not.
